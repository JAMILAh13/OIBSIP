## Objective

The objective of this task was to research and analyse common social engineering attack techniques, focusing on how attackers manipulate human behaviour rather than exploiting technical vulnerabilities.

The report examines:

- Phishing
- Spear Phishing
- Whaling
- Vishing
- Smishing
- Pretexting
- Baiting
- Quid Pro Quo

It also includes real-world case studies, prevention strategies, organisational security recommendations, and a comparison of different social engineering attack methods.

## Learning Outcomes

Through completing this project, I learned:

- How social engineering attacks exploit human psychology.
- The differences between phishing, pretexting, baiting, and quid pro quo attacks.
- The importance of employee security awareness.
- Why human behaviour remains one of the biggest cybersecurity risks.
- How organisations can reduce social engineering attacks through education, policies, and technical controls.
